# Channels

Social builds reach and free sign-ups, not ticket sales: judge it on reach. Sales come from search, email, partners and recommendations, mostly in the last four weeks, in working hours, on desktop. Write each channel for the job it does.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn (lead channel) | Reach professional audiences, partner reposts | Hook line under 12 words, then 2 to 4 short paragraphs or a three-line list. Name the audience in the first line. | One link with Climb27 UTM tags to the audience page |
| Instagram (second) | Short video, atmosphere | Caption hook under 8 words, one sentence of context, the CTA. Let the video carry it. | "Link in bio" plus the audience page |
| TikTok | Short video, ticket sales through TikTok Shop | On-screen text 3 to 6 words per beat. Caption one line. | Ticket link in the Shop |

| Press release | Give journalists a clear, usable news story | Factual headline, supporting lines, news lead, evidence, approved quotes, next step, Notes to editors. Read the Press writing section below. | One relevant information or participation link, plus a media contact |

## UTM tags

Every link to an audience page carries `utm_source` (the platform), `utm_medium` (social, email, partner, paid), `utm_campaign=climb27` and `utm_content` (the post or asset name). Never post a bare audience-page link.

# Press writing

Write a release so a journalist can understand the news quickly and reuse the facts. Keep the festival's confidence, but let the news and evidence carry the energy.

### Choose the story

Lead with one confirmed development and explain what it changes for founders or the people the story serves. An investor announcement can lead a release when it explains a useful opportunity for founders. It need not follow the headline order of a flyer.

Describe a host city as a platform for businesses across the UK where that is the story. Keep relevant history in the background. Lead with an evidenced track record and the next opportunity, rather than defining the festival only by where it started. Do not claim success without evidence.

Explain each organisation's role. Distinguish a new visiting conference or delegation from the established network that already contributes to the festival. Use the partner's confirmed name and describe what it is bringing. Network membership, past attendance and a forecast for a future event are different measures. Never combine them or turn one into another.

### Build the release

1. Write a factual headline: who is doing what, and why it matters. Use a complete thought. The short billboard headline rule does not apply to press releases.
2. Add one or two short supporting lines only when they add new facts. Do not repeat the headline or use the tagline as padding.
3. Put the main news, approved location, date and reader benefit in the opening paragraph. Give enough context for someone who has never heard of ClimbUK.
4. Explain the new development, then the practical experience: what someone can do, who is involved and why it helps. Put the strongest evidence early.
5. Use approved quotes to explain the purpose or show a real outcome. Keep each quote focused. A founder's experience can show the value to other founders more clearly than another organisational endorsement.
6. Close with one relevant next step. Put media contact details and concise organisation descriptions under Notes to editors. Keep product history, biographies and secondary logistics there unless they are central to the news.

Write short, complete paragraphs in third person outside quotes. Each paragraph should make sense if lifted on its own, and the story should still work if an editor cuts the final paragraphs. Use the length the news needs; do not pad a release to meet a word minimum.

### Sound confident and useful

Use an optimistic, grounded tone. Show opportunity through specific actions and evidence. Prefer verbs such as host, bring, connect, support and showcase to strings of abstract claims. Explain specialist terms on first use. Keep partner names, job titles and programme names exact.

Let quotes carry personal conviction and warmth. Keep the surrounding reporting calm and factual. Festival details add character when they explain the experience; they should not crowd out the business story. Avoid repeated mission statements, long lists of attractions and a sales pitch in every paragraph.

Ticket prices, discount codes, release urgency and sales language belong in a release only when they are material to the news or necessary for participation. A press release is not a partner sales leaflet. State the action and use a clear link; keep the fuller offer on its destination page.

### Protect evidence and approvals

- Use only confirmed, cleared facts. Leave unsigned deals and unconfirmed roles out of publishable copy. A discussion, a draft and an enthusiastic reply are not publication approval.
- Check every person's current title, each organisation's spelling and the precise scope of its involvement. Check facts even when the draft came from a specialist agency.
- Reuse an approved statement verbatim only within the permission given. Approval for one statement is not approval for new claims or a rewritten quote.
- Use real, consented quotes. A press release does not need the three-quote layout used for marketing testimonial panels. Choose the voices that add evidence or perspective. Use whole approved sentences when shortening; seek fresh approval for edits that change wording or meaning.
- Preserve approved quoted speech exactly, including its natural phrasing. House slogans, naming preferences and sales-language rules govern our narration, not silent edits to someone else's approved words. Quotes still need checks for factual accuracy, consent and embargoes.
- Use figures only when cleared for publication in that release or already published by ClimbUK. Record the source, period, definition and approval. Keep historic evidence separate from future plans. Never imply a guaranteed meeting, investment or outcome for the reader.
- Keep draft labels, missing quote briefs and approval questions in the review copy. Remove or resolve them before release. Use a release date or embargo statement that matches the agreed communications plan.

### Coordinate the announcement

Agree the media release time and public launch before publishing any channel. Protect the freshness of the story: newsletters, web pages, partner posts and social posts must respect the same embargo. Where the campaign plan requires advance press access, keep that lead time; a general writing guide does not shorten it.

Keep one approved master release, its approved quotes, images, captions and credits. Give marketing and partners the final version, then adapt it for their channels without adding unsupported claims. If a fact changes, update all affected versions and record the new approval. A supplied photograph needs an accurate caption, credit and permission to use it.

### Review before release

Confirm that the headline contains actual news, the opening explains the benefit, and each paragraph adds something new. Read once for tone and plain language. Read again for names, roles, figures, quotes and approvals. Check the release, covering email and images against the same master and launch plan. A spelling or style check cannot establish that a claim is true or cleared.

### Show why the news matters beyond the event

Explain the evidenced benefit to businesses, communities or the region where it belongs in the story. Keep the actual announcement in the lead. A public-interest angle strengthens relevant news; it does not require every release to open with a general social problem, or to claim an impact that has not been established. Source external figures to the primary publisher.

### Send a usable covering email

Use the headline as the subject. State what the release is and its release time, give two short sentences on its news value, list the attached Word and PDF release, captioned photos and logos, then give the agreed press contacts, a sign-off and an opt-out line. Send one email per newsroom, with attachments below 10 MB. Keep the same approved facts and embargo in the covering email and release. This is a preparation standard, not permission to send.
