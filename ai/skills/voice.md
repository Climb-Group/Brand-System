# ClimbUK brand voice

Use this skill whenever you write or edit words a customer, partner, attendee or the public will read for ClimbUK, ClimbGroup or a Climb edition (Climb27 and later): web pages, emails, social posts, ads, captions, scripts, slides, press releases, signage, merch lines, alt text, quotations and chatter replies that go to customers.

ClimbUK is business with a party, not a party for business: a festival-energy event brand for growing companies, the people who fund them and the firms that help them. Every line is exciting and useful.

## The voice in four lines

1. Headline energy. Short, rhythmic, confident, like a festival poster or a keynote slide.
2. Doing, not dreaming. Move the reader to action: name the outcome and the next step.
3. Curated, not crowded. Sell the right people in the room and the quality of conversations. Never sell size. Say what makes them the right people for the reader, rather than repeating a list of segments.
4. Personal, not generic. Write for one audience. If it could be about any event, rewrite it.

## Where the name comes from

"Climb" comes from the Investor Ladder: a business climbs to the next rung, an investor who fits where it is now, and investors pass businesses up and down the ladder to the rung that suits them. Use the story when someone asks about the name. ClimbUK is a business event, never a sport-climbing or outdoors event: use "climb" and "climbing" only when the business meaning is clear in the same line, never climbing-sport words or images (ropes, walls, harnesses, outdoor gear, "climbers" for attendees), and add growth, investors or the next stage when a line leads on "climb" alone.

## Hard rules

- No emojis, no exclamation marks, no em or en dashes. Plain hyphens only.
- Never "Climb" on its own. Write ClimbUK (the brand), ClimbGroup (the company, for legal lines and corporate pieces only), or the edition (Climb27, Climb26). The app is the "ClimbGroup Event App". Never explain who runs ClimbUK.
- Venue embargo: until it is announced, name no venue, city area, venue type or size, and do not hint at one. The word "venue" and "somewhere new" are banned. The check copy tool knows whether the embargo is on.
- No internal numbers: no attendance targets, "thousands", audience size, partner counts or late-booking percentages. Channel reach is the exception: partner materials may show the reach of ClimbUK's own channels (newsletter readers, monthly website and social views) and the share of attendees who follow up after meeting a partner, taken from the source data and written as a floor with a plus ("40k+ monthly newsletter readers"). Never an attendance figure, an audience size for the event or a promised number of meetings.
- No promises we cannot keep: no guarantees, never a number of investor meetings, introductions or funding. "Find investors looking for real deal flow" is fine; never "meet investors".
- Scarcity only when it is true. Name the ticket release, its price and its state. No "sold out", "last few" or "selling fast" unless the shop says so. No "premier", unsupported superlatives or trademark symbols.
- No hedges: no "TBC", "coming soon", "subject to", "we hope", "we never". State what happens.
- Real people only. Never invent a quote, a name or a company.
- Credit each quote to a named person and their organisation, never a company alone or a line such as "Climb26 exhibitor". Print their words exactly.
- For paying in instalments, name the providers and say plainly that any fee is passed on to the buyer and added to their order.
- The smallest stand is the "Startup pod", never "starter pod".
- Credit quotes with the person and organisation only, never the edition or year, and skip quotes that name an old edition.
- No vendor or platform names in customer copy.
- Big moments, not keynotes. Clinics are roundtables or workshops.
- No reports we have not agreed: never promise partners or sponsors an impact report, a post-event report or audience data. Sell what happens in the room.

## Headlines and titles

- Open positive, above all on flyers, banners and posters: say what ClimbUK is, never what it is not. Never "Not a conference".
- A title names the benefit, not the slot: "Connections that lead to work", not "New partners". Leave out points that are not a reason to come, such as booking in the app.
- Peers are people one step ahead who give advice ("Advice from one step ahead"), not "founders at your stage".
- The investor side can be mentioned but is never the main point. "The Investor Ladder" is not a title on its own.
- ClimbUK supports the whole funding journey: never "up to Series A".
- Only the grand opening has live music: never sell music or DJ sets as a reason to come.

## Words we use

agenda (not programme, for the schedule); Affiliate Programme (not referral scheme); public sector (not councils); Partner Manager (a job title, capitalised); connect you with, bring you (not introduce you to, matched); fractional leaders are not consultants; fellow founders, peers one step ahead; find investors looking for real deal flow, investors here to find their next deal (not meet investors, investors ready to invest). A heading always carries a benefit.

## Lines we own

- Company tagline: The Festival for Business Growth. "The Festival of Business Growth" is the established spoken form and is also correct; use one form per piece.
  In a sentence it is a description, so it is lower case: "Climb27 is the festival for business growth." Capitals only when the tagline stands on its own.
- Web addresses on print and PDFs are written in full with www: www.climb-uk.com/partnerships, never "climb-uk.com/partnerships". Email addresses stay as they are.
- Describe a zone or a format by what people do there (mentoring, meeting, discussing, pitching), not by its furniture or how many there are: write "Advisers and fractional leaders mentoring founders and talking through what comes next", never "Advisers and fractional leaders, in 24 pods".
- Subheadings and straplines say something concrete: who it is for or what they get. Never a pair of clever opposites that could describe anything ("Small by design. Powerful by intent.", "Big enough to be seen. Sharp enough to close.").
- Climb27 headline: Your business. Bigger stage.
- General Climb27 flyer headline: Bring your ambition. Find your people. Accelerate your growth.
- Themes: Find Your People, and Main Stage Moments.

## Clear for everyone

- Links say where they go. Never "click here", "read more", "learn more" or a bare "here".
- Spell out abbreviations on first use, except common ones (UK, AI, CEO, SME, FAQ, PDF and similar).
- Aim for reading grade 9 or lower: short sentences, everyday words.
- Link unusual words to the glossary at /glossary, or explain them in the sentence.

## How to write a piece

1. Pick the audience and the channel. Call the audience tool and the channel tool for their tone, needs, shape and call to action.
2. Write the headline first: 3 to 8 words, one idea, a two or three beat rhythm.
3. Write short, complete paragraphs: what happens, why it matters to this reader, what to do next.
4. End with one call to action that names the action ("Get your ticket", "Book your meetings").
5. Call the check copy tool on the full text. Fix every problem, act on the hints, then check again until it passes.
6. Use only facts (dates, prices, formats, speakers) that the person gave you or that are in the record you are working on. Never invent them.

When a piece has a copy file (a Markdown file of its words, with each part of the design marked `<!-- @front.headline -->`), write and change its words in that file, keep every marker and note as it is, and never move words into the design itself.
